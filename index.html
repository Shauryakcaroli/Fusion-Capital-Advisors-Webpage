Sector Note Q1 2026 · 8 min read

India's A&D Indigenisation: The Inbound Capital Window

European primes and Japanese strategics are recalibrating their India strategies in real-time. The structural opportunity for inbound capital is now — and the window will not stay open indefinitely.

By Vinmre Anand & Rajneesh Sehgal Fusion Capital Advisors

The setup

Three forces are converging on India's aerospace and defence value chain. First, sustained government commitment to indigenisation through the Defence Acquisition Procedure and the Strategic Partnership model has shifted procurement preference from imports to domestically-manufactured systems. Second, global primes — Airbus, Leonardo, Boeing, Mitsubishi Heavy Industries — are under pressure to localise content to retain access to one of the world's fastest-growing defence budgets. Third, the supplier base in India has matured beyond simple build-to-print into engineering-led manufacturing across precision components, structural assemblies, and avionics.

The result is a cycle of inbound capital formation that has only just begun. Our 2024 mandate to Poggiopolini Group on its India market entry through Aero Fasteners reflects this thesis at work: an Italian Tier-1 supplier seeking a domestic manufacturing footprint to participate in India's aerospace supply chain at the front end of the cycle.

Where capital is moving

We see three clear capital-formation patterns. Acquisitions of established Indian Tier-1 and Tier-2 suppliers by European primes seeking immediate scale — typically valued on revenue-multiple or DCF basis given long-cycle order books. Joint ventures and minority investments by Japanese strategics with longer time horizons and a preference for technology-transfer-led partnerships. And greenfield majority partnerships structured as the Poggiopolini-Aero Fasteners transaction, where a European principal takes control of a domestic platform to build manufacturing capability from the ground up.

The medium-term thesis

The component-supply consolidation phase is well underway. The next eighteen to twenty-four months will see structural assemblies and avionics joint-venture formation — typically larger, more complex transactions with deeper technology-transfer components and longer execution timelines. Deal sizes will scale from the current sub-USD 50M typical envelope to USD 100-300M as the platforms underpinning these JVs require greater operational sophistication.

What we advise sponsors

Three practical considerations. First, regulatory clearance pathways are non-trivial and must be modelled into transaction timelines from term-sheet stage — not as a post-signing diligence item. Second, intellectual property structuring requires deliberate attention given India's IP regime peculiarities for defence-classified technology. Third, operational integration in the post-closing phase is where most cross-border A&D transactions stumble; advisor continuity from term-sheet through 100-day plan is materially valuable.

The window is not closed. But it is narrower than the deal flow numbers would suggest.

Where we have conviction

Precision fasteners, structural sub-assemblies, defence electronics, and MRO-adjacent component manufacturing remain our highest-conviction sub-segments. We continue to advise on inbound mandates with Italian, French, and Japanese principals, with active engagements totalling more than a billion in combined enterprise value across the live pipeline.

Deal Trend Q4 2025 · 6 min read

MBO Structures in Indian Family Businesses

Succession-driven control transactions in India's mid-market consumer and industrials sectors are accelerating. The Management Buyout model is no longer a private-equity preserve — Indian corporate acquirers are now its most enthusiastic adopters.

By Vinmre Anand & Rajneesh Sehgal Fusion Capital Advisors

Why now

India's first generation of post-liberalisation entrepreneurs is approaching an inflection point. Founders who built businesses through the 1990s and early 2000s are reckoning with succession decisions in earnest, and the question of "who runs the business after me" is reshaping deal structuring in the mid-market. Where private equity once dominated the buyout conversation, Indian corporate strategics with capital and a category-extension thesis are now equally credible counterparties.

Our 2019 mandate on the Babaji Snacks transfer of control to a billion-dollar Indian food & snacks group — structured as a Management Buyout retaining the executive team with share ownership while providing exit to non-contributing family owners — was an early example of what is now becoming a recognisable pattern.

The MBO playbook for family businesses

Three ingredients consistently appear. Continuity of operating leadership — the executive team continues post-closing, typically with rolled equity and clearly defined performance milestones. Exit liquidity for non-contributing family owners — those without operational involvement are bought out cleanly, removing future governance friction. Strategic acquirer or sponsor capital — providing the equity cheque, the governance overlay, and ongoing growth capital.

What's changed in the past three years is the third ingredient. Indian corporates with proven category execution and capital availability are now winning these processes against private-equity bidders, particularly when the seller prioritises strategic continuity over headline price.

Where the model fits best

Mid-market consumer brands with regional distribution strength, mid-market industrials with proven cash generation, and family-owned businesses where the second generation is uninterested in operational succession — these are the highest-fit categories. The model becomes harder when the founder remains operationally critical and there is no credible successor inside the executive team.

Indian owners are now less emotional about retaining majority ownership in value-enhancing collaborative structures with trade partners and private equity funds.

What sponsors should expect

Process timelines for family-business MBOs typically run 7-9 months from mandate to close, materially longer than standard PE buyouts because of the relationship and trust-building requirement. Valuation expectations are increasingly anchored to strategic-acquirer benchmarks rather than financial-buyer multiples, particularly for businesses with category leadership in their geography.

Market Commentary Q4 2025 · 5 min read

Cross-Border Mid-Market: Where the Deals Are Closing

The India-Italy and India-Japan industrial corridors continue to outperform headline cross-border M&A volumes. Specialist component manufacturing remains the most active sub-segment, and the pattern is structural rather than cyclical.

By Vinmre Anand & Rajneesh Sehgal Fusion Capital Advisors

The pattern

Headline cross-border M&A volumes into India have been mixed across 2024 and 2025. But beneath the headline, two corridors have continued to deliver consistent transaction flow: India-Italy in industrials and aerospace, and India-Japan in automotive components and precision engineering. Both corridors share characteristics that explain their resilience — established corporate familiarity, complementary manufacturing capabilities, and aligned long-term strategic intent on both sides.

Why Italy

Italian industrial groups bring engineering depth in precision components, aerospace structures, and specialist machinery — all categories where India is scaling rapidly but lacks the heritage know-how. Italian principals have shown willingness to commit to majority structures with operating control, which simplifies execution. The Poggiopolini-Aero Fasteners transaction we advised in 2024 reflects this dynamic in its purest form.

Why Japan

Japanese strategics historically operate on longer time horizons than Western buyout sponsors, which aligns well with the multi-year integration cycles required in Indian manufacturing. Joint-venture and minority structures are preferred, with technology transfer and operational know-how flowing in exchange for market access. Automotive component manufacturers — particularly in EV-adjacent and specialist machining segments — have been the most active sub-category.

What's not closing

Generalist financial-buyer cross-border activity has slowed. Inflated valuation expectations from Indian sellers, combined with rising cost-of-capital headwinds for foreign sponsors, have taken many "would-be" deals out of contention. Deals that close are increasingly strategic in nature, where buyer commitment is anchored to specific operational synergies rather than multiple arbitrage.

Strategic cross-border transactions are closing. Financial cross-border transactions are not.

Outlook into 2026

We expect continued deal flow in the Italy-India and Japan-India corridors, with potential acceleration in two specific sub-segments: aerospace structural assemblies and EV-adjacent automotive components. France-India and Germany-India industrial activity should reaccelerate as European corporates complete current restructuring cycles. The US-India corridor is harder to call given political and regulatory uncertainty, but mid-market industrial transactions in metals and specialty chemicals should continue.
